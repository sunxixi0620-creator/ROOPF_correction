# Frozen component study: manuscript-ready result and limits

On new instances from the development task distribution, the fixed-window learned prior reduced restricted target-hitting time relative to an online GP, a shuffled-training prior, and space-filling initialization by 15.83, 14.05, and 17.11 evaluations, respectively. Adding online adaptation also improved terminal bounded utility over frozen-prior-only guidance by 0.02987. These findings support early search benefits and the contribution of online adaptation under the studied conditions.

However, the learned prior did not outperform a matched analytic radial prior: its mean time saving was -2.08 evaluations, with an adjusted interval spanning zero. Thus, improvement over the online and shuffled-prior controls does not establish that neural pretraining is necessary. Fixed-window withdrawal also did not improve target-hitting efficiency over persistent fusion. Target attainment at 300 evaluations remained lower than that of space-filling initialization, so the strict combined criterion was not met.

The primary target, budget, and component criteria were frozen before the runs. Restricted time includes unsuccessful trajectories, assigned the prespecified truncation value of 301 rather than omitted. The intervals adjust six comparisons within each endpoint and cluster by twelve task recipes; they are not simultaneous coverage over all endpoints. The task families have prior development exposure. These results neither establish generalization to untouched task families nor validate the original ROOPF anchor or residual selector.
